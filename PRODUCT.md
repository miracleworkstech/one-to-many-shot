## Platform

web

## Users

Ellie is the primary user. She runs product content for a six-person home-goods brand, along with half of everything else, and she reviews on her phone between other things: one thumb, a few seconds per image, deciding whether a candidate shot matches the idea in the sheet. She refused to install anything new and stopped logging in to the last tool after a week, so this has to open from a shared link and be obvious on first use.

Maya, the founder, is the secondary user. She wants to see where the 40-product drop stands and what has been spent without asking Ellie. The web person is a third, occasional user who downloads the approved images and the updated CSV.

## Product Purpose

Turns the shot ideas in the team's catalog spreadsheet into styled product images, generated by Luma, approved by Ellie, and exported back as files and an updated CSV. It replaces the reconstruct-the-wishlist, email-the-photographer, forward-to-Slack loop with one place that knows which requests are done. Success is the drop launching with approved styled shots, no budget spent on images nobody wanted, and nobody having to ask which file is final.

## Positioning

See where the drop stands at a glance, approve in a tap. Every generation shows its price before the trigger and is capped per run, which is the claim the abandoned dashboard could not make.

## Operating Context

The team's whole toolkit is Google Sheets, Slack and Gmail. The catalog is one shared sheet, one row per product, with a Shot Idea column. Maya exports it as CSV and new exports keep coming, so the CSV is the entry point and an updated CSV plus a zip of approved images is the exit. The app opens from a shared link with a token; visiting it once sets a cookie. Review happens on Ellie's phone, mid-task. Maya checks status on whatever she has open. Generation runs against the Luma API, costs real money per image, and a Slack message announces settled batches. The first real test is a 40-product drop landing next month.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Import the catalog CSV, keep existing approvals on re-import, and suggest a shot idea where the sheet has none.
- Generate a bounded batch of candidates for the next N products, or for one product, with the estimated cost on the button and caps on images in flight and total spend.
- Review per SKU: candidate images, approve or reject, edit the shot idea, try again with a note. Prev and next move through the catalog.
- Status page: counts per product status, spend summary, recent batches, a paused banner with a resume action when the worker stops.
- Export the updated CSV and a zip of approved images.
- Scale ceiling: about 300 products, drops of 40. Single SQLite database on a Railway volume, one worker.
- Terminology is the team's: "shot idea", "candidates", "approved", "the drop". Not "assets", "workflows" or "pipelines".
- Undecided: nothing beyond the current two screens has been scoped for the interface.

## Brand Commitments

The interface should feel editorial, calm and trustworthy. It reads like a well-edited contact sheet, not a control room. The images are the loudest thing on the review page and the chrome around them is quiet. Money and status are stated plainly in words and numbers, never dramatised.

Binding reference: the Apple Photos review flow. The image fills the width, the decisions are big thumb targets directly under it, and moving to the next item is one gesture.

Anti-references, all confirmed:

- The creative-automation dashboard the team abandoned after a week: KPI tiles, charts, gradients, a login nobody returned to.
- A generic SaaS admin template: sidebar navigation, card grids, identical icon-heading-text blocks.
- A raw spreadsheet clone: dense rows and cells. The sheet is the input, not the experience.

## Evidence on Hand

- The customer's real export at `data/catalog.csv`, a sample of the full 300 with quirks intact.
- The brief in `README.md`, with the team's own words about their process and what they rejected.
- No testimonials, logos or press exist and none should be invented.
- The Luma account had zero credits as of 2026-09-04, so real generated images are not on hand yet.

## Product Principles

- Status before action. Every screen answers "where does this stand" before it offers a button.
- The image is the interface. On the review page, chrome yields to the candidate; decisions sit under it within thumb reach.
- Money is always visible. No generation trigger without its price on the button and the cap in view.
- Their words, not ours. Labels and messages use the team's vocabulary and say what happened in plain English.
- Nothing to learn. First use from a shared link on a phone, no onboarding, no settings to find.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text at 4.5:1 or better, visible focus states, every control labelled, tap targets of at least 44 px (already in place on the review page), and every animation respects prefers-reduced-motion.
